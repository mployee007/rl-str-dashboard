# NVIDIA Q2 FY2027 Earnings Transcript — Bull/Bear Debate Sheet

## Snapshot
| Item | Value |
|---|---:|
| Q2 revenue | $96.2B |
| Data Center revenue | $89.0B |
| Q3 guide | $108.0B ±2% |
| FY2028 growth guide | ~70% YoY |
| Q3 GM guide | 74.0% ±50 bps |
| Q4 GM view | 71%–72% trough |
| FY2028 GM view | 72%–73% |

## Core debate
**Bull case:** NVIDIA remains the bottlenecked center of the AI build-out, with demand meaningfully above supply and growth broadening beyond hyperscalers.

**Bear case:** The business is still exceptional, but margin pressure, supply constraints, geopolitical exclusions, and eventual platform competition could cap upside versus elevated expectations.

---

## Bull case
| Argument | Evidence from call | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Demand is stronger than the reported guide | Management said FY2028 growth of ~70% is supply-constrained and demand is higher | Suggests estimates may still be limited by execution, not demand |
| Broadening customer base | ACIE hit $40B, up 25% QoQ and 138% YoY | Growth is no longer just a hyperscaler concentration story |
| AI spending is monetizing | Jensen argued tokens are productive and profitable | Supports sustained customer ROI and continued capex intensity |
| Vera Rubin could be another major step-up | Production started; management expects fastest ramp in company history | Next product cycle may extend leadership and lift revenue density |
| Full-stack platform advantage | NVIDIA positioned itself across GPU, CPU, networking, software, and systems | Raises switching costs and expands wallet share |
| Revenue per GW is rising | Hopper ~$18B/GW, Grace Blackwell ~$25B/GW, Vera Rubin ~$40B/GW | Suggests improving monetization of finite infrastructure footprints |
| Networking and CPU upside are additive | Record networking quarter; server CPU revenue expected to more than double in FY2028 | Investors may still undervalue non-GPU contribution |
| China absence makes guide cleaner | No China data center compute revenue assumed in forward outlook | Reduces risk that guidance depends on geopolitically fragile business |

## Bear case
| Argument | Evidence from call | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Margins are rolling over | Q3 GM guided to 74%; Q4 seen at 71%–72% | At this scale, even modest margin compression matters a lot |
| Memory is capturing scarcity economics too | Management cited extreme memory pricing conditions | Supplier power can blunt incremental operating leverage |
| Supply constraints may frustrate upside | Company cannot meet full demand today | Investors may pay for demand NVIDIA cannot physically ship |
| Infrastructure bottlenecks are broader than chips | Jensen cited land, power, shell, cooling, labor, and foundry constraints | Limits near-term conversion of backlog into recognized revenue |
| AI lab / custom chip risk persists | Frontier labs remain strategic customers, but some are designing custom silicon | Over time, custom accelerators could pressure NVIDIA share or pricing |
| Financing structures may add complexity | NVIDIA discussed take-or-pay and revenue-sharing frameworks with neoclouds | Could trigger concerns about cyclicality, credit exposure, or quality of demand |
| China remains a dead zone | Less than 1% of Q2 data center revenue came from China; none assumed forward | Lost optionality matters if domestic competition strengthens elsewhere |
| Expectations are already extreme | After multiple years of hypergrowth, the market may require near-perfect execution | Great results can still underwhelm if the bar stays too high |

---

## Key questions for the next quarter
1. How fast does Vera Rubin ramp relative to management’s “fastest in history” framing?
2. Does ACIE remain near half of Data Center and keep growing faster than hyperscale?
3. Are memory costs stabilizing, or does the gross margin trough deepen?
4. Can NVIDIA unlock enough supply to move beyond the current ~70% FY2028 framing?
5. Do revenue-sharing / financing structures begin contributing meaningfully, or mostly add narrative complexity?

## What would make the bull case stronger
- Q3 beats guide with better-than-feared margins
- Vera Rubin ramps faster than expected
- Supply constraints visibly ease
- ACIE remains very strong without credit concerns
- Additional large hyperscaler or sovereign commitments arrive

## What would make the bear case stronger
- Margins fall below the guided trough range
- Memory pricing worsens again
- Vera Rubin ramp slips or creates mix disruption
- Infrastructure bottlenecks delay deployments
- Custom-chip narratives start showing real share loss in inference

## My scorecard
| Dimension | Read |
|---|---|
| Demand | Strongly bullish |

| Margins | Near-term negative, medium-term manageable |
| Competitive position | Still dominant |
| Visibility | Better than prior quarters |
| Overall setup | Positive, but expectations remain unforgiving |

## Closing view
If you are arguing the **bull case**, the strongest point is simple: NVIDIA is guiding extraordinary growth while still saying it cannot meet all the demand in front of it.

If you are arguing the **bear case**, the strongest point is that when expectations are already extraordinary, supply limits and margin compression can still compress the stock’s upside even if the business keeps performing.
