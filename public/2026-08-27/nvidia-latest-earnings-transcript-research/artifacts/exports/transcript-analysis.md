# NVIDIA Q2 FY2027 Earnings Transcript — Research Brief

## Bottom line
NVIDIA's latest earnings call was **decisively bullish on demand** and **candidly cautious on supply and margins**.

The central message from management:
1. **Demand is running well above what NVIDIA can currently supply.**
2. **FY2028 revenue is expected to grow ~70% year over year, but that is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained.**
3. **The next leg of growth is broadening beyond hyperscalers** into sovereign AI, neoclouds, enterprises, and AI labs.
4. **Gross margin will compress near term** because of extreme memory pricing, then partially recover after price increases.

## What mattered most in the transcript

### 1) The story is no longer “AI demand is strong.” It is “AI compute is already monetizing.”
Jensen Huang's most important framing was that AI has crossed from experimentation into economically productive deployment:
- “AI is now doing productive and useful work.”
- “AI is generating profitable tokens.”
- More compute directly translates into more revenue for customers.

That matters because management is arguing the capex cycle is becoming **self-funding**, not speculative.

### 2) NVIDIA is steering investors to look beyond hyperscalers
Colette Kress split Data Center into:
- **Hyperscale:** $49B, up 13% sequentially
- **ACIE:** $40B, up 25% sequentially and 138% YoY

ACIE includes **neocloud, industrial, enterprise, sovereign, edge, and air-gapped infrastructure**. Management emphasized that this “other half” is underappreciated and may become larger over time than the hyperscaler opportunity alone.

### 3) FY2028 growth could be higher if supply were available
Management said customer forecasts point to growth **doubling next year**, but NVIDIA only guided to roughly **70%** because supply remains the bottleneck through at least FY2028.

Jensen's message in Q&A was blunt:
- demand is “much greater than 70%”
- the whole supply chain is stretched
- land, power, shell, memory, foundry, cooling, and labor all matter now

This is a major signal that investors should read FY2028 guidance as a **floor shaped by execution capacity**, not a ceiling shaped by end demand.

### 4) Near-term margin pressure is real and memory-driven
This was the main “less bullish” piece of the call.

Colette said:
- memory prices have risen more than expected
- pricing conditions are “extreme”
- Q3 gross margin should be ~74%
- margins may trough in Q4 at 71%–72%
- FY2028 should stabilize at 72%–73% once price increases take effect

Interpretation: NVIDIA is still printing enormous earnings, but the supply chain is capturing some of the AI scarcity economics too.

### 5) Vera Rubin is the next major ramp, and management expects it to be the fastest in company history
Management said:
- Vera Rubin production shipments began earlier this month
- every major hyperscaler, AI cloud, and OEM has already placed purchase orders
- Vera Rubin could be the **fastest ramp in NVIDIA history**
- Vera Rubin should be ~20% of data center revenue in Q3

They also argued that revenue opportunity per gigawatt has climbed:
- Hopper: ~ $18B/GW
- Grace Blackwell: ~ $25B/GW
- Vera Rubin: ~ $40B/GW

That was a recurring theme: NVIDIA wants investors to think in terms of **economics per gigawatt** and **AI factory yield**, not just unit chip shipments.

## Notable Q&A takeaways

### Joseph Moore (Morgan Stanley): Why guide 70%, and why now?
**Takeaway:** Management believes visibility is now strong enough across both upstream and downstream constraints to guide a year ahead.

Jensen said the company now works not just with chip and memory suppliers, but also with:
- power providers
- land / power / shell developers
- broader infrastructure partners

That wider visibility is what gives them confidence in the 70% supply-backed growth number.

### C.J. Muse (Cantor): What happens to inference share as workloads become more agentic?
**Takeaway:** NVIDIA argued complexity plays in its favor.

Jensen framed AI as a four-phase lifecycle:
- data prep / synthetic data
- pre-training
- post-training
- agentic inference

His argument: rack-scale NVIDIA systems are valuable because the same platform can serve all phases and many model types, preserving customer investment and raising switching costs.

### Stacy Rasgon (Bernstein): What is driving the step-up in growth and how much higher would it be unconstrained?
**Takeaway:** Management said the “invisible” ACIE opportunity is the big underappreciated growth driver.

Jensen emphasized that many non-hyperscale buyers do not want component-level procurement. They want the whole AI factory stack. That is where NVIDIA believes it creates the most differentiated value.

### Vivek Arya (BofA): Why invest in frontier labs that are also designing custom chips?
**Takeaway:** NVIDIA is not positioning itself as just a chip vendor competing SKU-to-SKU.

Jensen's answer was that NVIDIA offers a full platform spanning training, post-training, inference, and global deployment across clouds. He said he remains highly confident those AI labs will remain major NVIDIA customers for a long time even while pursuing custom silicon.

### Ben Reitzes (Melius): What do recursive self-improvement and AGI-like systems mean for demand?
**Takeaway:** Jensen sees agentic AI as a structural demand multiplier.

His clearest claim: companies may eventually have orders of magnitude more AI agents than human employees, and those agents run continuously. That implies far higher steady-state compute demand than chat-style usage alone.

### Jim Schneider (Goldman): What are the real bottlenecks?
**Takeaway:** It is not one chokepoint.

Jensen said the **entire supply chain is challenged**. That includes memory, manufacturing, and infrastructure readiness. Capacity is improving continuously, but not fast enough to catch demand yet.

## My read
**Verdict: strongly positive transcript, with the only real soft spot being margin compression from memory scarcity.**

The call did not read like a company defending a peak. It read like a company telling investors:
- demand is broadening, not narrowing
- compute is becoming a revenue asset for customers
- the next bottleneck is physical capacity, not buyer appetite
- NVIDIA is moving up the stack from chips to full AI factory economics

If you want the single-sentence takeaway:

> NVIDIA's latest earnings transcript says demand is still outrunning supply, the opportunity is widening beyond hyperscalers, and near-term margin pressure is the price of owning the bottlenecked center of the AI build-out.
