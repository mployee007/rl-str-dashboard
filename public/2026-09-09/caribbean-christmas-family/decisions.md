# Decisions & Methodology

## Ranking methodology
1. **Value Score (40%)**: Flight affordability + lodging cost at Christmas. Favor large-inventory markets.
2. **Luxury Vibe (30%)**: Resort quality, beach aesthetics, dining, perceived sophistication for dollar.
3. **Family Fit (30%)**: Kids' clubs, safe swimming, activities, all-inclusive availability, ease of travel.

## Key decisions made

### Cancun/Riviera Maya ranks #1 despite not being "true Caribbean"
**Rationale:** The user values inexpensive lodging + airfare + luxurious vibe + family. Cancun has the largest resort inventory in the region, direct flights from Chicago, all-inclusives from $199/night (Hilton Tulum), and world-class amenities. The sheer supply dampens Christmas pricing relative to smaller islands. "Caribbean" is commonly understood to include the Riviera Maya in vacation marketing.

### Puerto Rico ranks #2 over Dominican Republic
**Rationale:** No passport requirement + cheapest flights + genuine culture (Old San Juan) + affordable local dining = better "luxurious feel per dollar" than DR all-inclusives. For a family that wants to explore beyond the resort, PR wins. DR all-inclusives are better for families who never want to leave the property.

### Curaçao included over Aruba
**Rationale:** Curaçao offers similar Dutch Caribbean charm at 20-30% less cost than Aruba. Same hurricane-belt safety. More authentic, less commercialized. Better value.

### Jamaica ranks lower than competitors at Christmas
**Rationale:** Sandals Royal Caribbean starts at $387/night — and that's the non-Christmas rate. Jamaica's Christmas premium is steep, and the "luxurious feel" costs more here than in Mexico or DR.

## Christmas pricing caveats
All prices in this report are indicative/source-derived estimates. Actual Christmas 2025 pricing must be checked on booking sites. The ranking assumes Christmas peak pricing applies to ALL destinations. Early December or post-New Year travel would yield dramatically better value (30-50% less) for any of these destinations.
