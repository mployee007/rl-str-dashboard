# Best ZIP Codes for Rental Property Investment — Toledo, Cleveland & Detroit

**Compiled:** September 2026  
**Data Source:** Live Zillow listings via Zillapi + Zillow benchmarks (ZHVI/ZORI) for suburbs where Zillapi credits were exhausted.  
**Method:** Median sale prices and rents computed from active for-sale/for-rent listings aggregated by ZIP, then ranked by gross yield (annual rent ÷ sale price).

---

## Bottom Line (One Paragraph)

All three cities deliver cash-flow yields that are virtually nonexistent in coastal markets. **Toledo** leads on raw headline yield (16–30% gross in value-add ZIPs), but the shallow rental market and thin buyer pool mean exits are slower. **Cleveland** offers the best balance of yield + institutional demand — strong Section 8 infrastructure, university/medical employment anchors, and zoning-friendly 2–4 unit stock. **Detroit** has the widest spread between goldilocks and cheap-trap ZIPs; the top 3 goldilocks (Corktown/Woodbridge, Eastern Market, Midtown) are priced for 8–13% gross with real job/population tailwinds, while sub-$50K ZIPs are investor graveyards.

## Direct Recommendations

1. **If you're buying one property today:** Cleveland 44129 (Parma W) — $190K, $1,950/mo rent, 12.3% gross yield, stable B-class suburb, lowest management headache of any high-yield ZIP across all three cities.

2. **If you're building a portfolio of 5+ doors:** Detroit 48208 (Corktown) for cash flow + appreciation; Cleveland 44103/44104 for raw yield; Toledo 43607/43613 for diversification at low basis.

3. **If you're doing Section 8 / voucher-heavy:** Cleveland 44103/44104/44105 have the best HCV infrastructure — CMHA pays ~110% of FMR and the program is well-administered.

4. **What to AVOID:** Detroit 48213/48211 (sub-$50K cheap-traps), Toledo 43604 (single-data-point outlier), Detroit 48226 (3% yield CBD door-count play).
