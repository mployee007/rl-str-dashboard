If your goal is **small residential BRRRR work in Rockford** — especially cosmetic-to-moderate rehab, rental-turn upgrades, basement refreshes, kitchens/baths, exterior repairs, and light layout/design coordination — the best local starting points from the live search were:

1. **New Leaf Remodeling** — best overall fit
2. **Creative Building & Remodeling** — good GC/remodel bid target
3. **Klarmann Construction & Remodeling** — good value-add rehab bid target
4. **City Construction & Design** — useful if scope needs design + GC coordination
5. **A-1 Quality Construction** — backup GC bid
6. **Twins Construction of Rockford** — backup labor-oriented GC bid
7. **Rock Valley Construction** — light-verification backup
8. **Mike Harris Masonry Contractor** — specialist add-on for brick/foundation/exterior risk

## What I found
The search stack did **not** surface many firms explicitly branding themselves as “BRRRR contractors” or “investor rehab specialists.” That’s normal in secondary Midwest markets. Instead, the best practical approach is to target local **residential remodelers / general contractors** whose published scope overlaps BRRRR needs, then screen them hard on:
- speed to start
- ability to work off a fixed scope / allowance schedule
- comfort with rental-grade finishes
- experience with older housing stock
- willingness to price repeat investor work

## How I’d use them for Longwood-style deals
For a Rockford deal like **417 N Longwood St**, I would not ask for a generic “estimate.” I would ask each contractor to price the same investor scope in 3 buckets:

### Bucket A — rental-turn / light rehab
- paint
- LVP / flooring patch + replace
- lighting / hardware / trim touch-up
- kitchen refresh (not full reconfigure)
- bath refresh
- minor exterior repair

### Bucket B — standard BRRRR rehab
- everything in A
- moderate electrical/plumbing corrections
- window/door replacements as needed
- cabinet/counter replacement
- drywall / water-damage repair
- exterior porch/step repair

### Bucket C — heavy rehab
- roof / foundation / masonry / major moisture items
- major HVAC/electrical/plumbing replacement
- layout changes
- extensive subfloor or framing repair

That gives you a clean way to compare contractors against your MAO grid.

## Investor screening questions
Ask every contractor these exact questions:
1. **Do you do investor-owned rental rehabs or only homeowner remodels?**
2. **Can you quote to a fixed scope with allowance line items?**
3. **What’s your earliest start date?**
4. **How many Rockford rehabs are you actively running right now?**
5. **Can you work in rental-grade finish tiers instead of owner-occupant finish tiers?**
6. **Do you handle permits and inspections, or only labor?**
7. **Can you price change orders in writing before doing the work?**
8. **Do you have references for older Rockford homes with masonry/moisture issues?**
9. **What trades are in-house vs subcontracted?**
10. **If I send repeat work, do you offer volume pricing or priority scheduling?**

## Red flags for BRRRR
Pass or de-prioritize a contractor if they:
- only want cost-plus with no scope discipline
- push owner-occupant finishes for rental deals
- can’t start for 6–8+ weeks on a small project
- refuse written change-order pricing
- won’t break out labor/material buckets
- don’t have any answer on older-home water/masonry issues
