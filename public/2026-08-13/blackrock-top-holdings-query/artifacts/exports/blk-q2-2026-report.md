## 2) Key risk factors / management discussion

### Main management points
- AUM growth was driven by **market appreciation plus inflows**.
- ETF flows were led by **core equity** and **index bond ETFs**.
- Retail flows were driven by **active fixed income**, **Aperio**, and **liquid alternatives**.
- Institutional active strength came from **private markets**, **fixed income**, **systematic equity**, and **outsourcing / target-date offerings**.
- Institutional index outflows were concentrated in **low-fee index equity offerings**.
- FX created a headwind, with AUM reduced by foreign-exchange movements as the U.S. dollar strengthened.

### Risk-factor read
In the latest 10-Q, BlackRock says the risks discussed in its Annual Report on Form 10-K for the year ended December 31, 2025 could materially affect the business, financial condition, operating results, and nonoperating results.

### Practical investor risk list
| Risk | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Market / AUM sensitivity | Lower markets can reduce AUM and fee revenue |
| Flow risk | Institutional or ETF outflows can slow growth |
| Fee compression | Passive/index pricing remains competitive |
| FX risk | Global AUM translation can move against results |
| Private-market execution risk | Expansion can improve growth but adds integration and complexity |
| Regulatory / political scrutiny | BlackRock’s size keeps it under heavy attention |
| Technology / platform risk | Aladdin and operating infrastructure are core to the model |
| Litigation / contingent liabilities | Large financial firms always carry legal and liability exposure |

### Clean interpretation
BLK remains a very strong business, but it is still highly tied to market levels, client flows, and fee-rate durability.
