# Retiring Abroad: Guyana vs. Mexico vs. Panama vs. Costa Rica
**Comparative Analysis — September 21, 2026**
*Prepared for Hermes Tompkins*

---

## VERDICT (TL;DR)

| Rank | Country | Best For… |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Panama** 🇵🇦 | The best all-around package: Pensionado visa, territorial tax, US dollar, modern healthcare, equal property rights |
| **2** | **Costa Rica** 🇨🇷 | Lowest income threshold, best healthcare, safest country, foreign income tax-free — but beachfront ownership is hard |
| **3** | **Mexico** 🇲🇽 | Largest expat community, most climate variety, cheapest day-to-day living — but highest visa threshold and IMSS excludes chronic conditions |
| **4** | **Guyana** 🇬🇾 | English-speaking and oil-boom upside — but no retiree visa, weak healthcare, high crime, and minimal expat infrastructure. Niche play only. |

**If you have a lifetime pension of $1,000+/month:** Panama or Costa Rica are the clear winners.
**If you're asset-rich but income-light:** Mexico's savings alternative ($74K) works. Costa Rica's $1,000 pensionado is still the easiest entry.
**If you prioritize being able to return to the US quickly and often:** Mexico (shortest flights, 50+ airports).
**Guyana:** Only compelling if you have existing family/business ties, want English as a first language, or are betting on the oil economy. For a typical US retiree, it's a distant fourth.

## 3. HEALTHCARE

|  | **Guyana** | **Mexico** | **Panama** | **Costa Rica** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WHO/UN ranking** | Not ranked (poor) | 61st | 95th | **36th (ahead of USA), #1 in Latin America** |

| **Monthly public cost (retiree)** | N/A | **~$63-126/mo** (IMSS) | ~$50-100 | **~$90-150** (income-based) |
| **Private hospitals (JCI-accredited)** | None | Hospital Ángeles, CIMA, Médica Sur | **Punta Pacífica (Johns Hopkins), San Fernando** | CIMA, Clínica Bíblica |
| **Private insurance (age 65)** | Medevac insurance required | $100-350/mo | $100-300/mo | $100-250/mo |

⚠️ **Critical Mexico IMSS caveat:** Explicitly excludes malignant tumors, chronic degenerative diseases (diabetes, hypertension, COPD, heart disease), HIV, and pre-existing conditions. Retirees with ANY chronic condition should budget for private insurance in Mexico.

## 5. TAXES

|  | **Guyana** | **Mexico** | **Panama** | **Costa Rica** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tax system** | Residence-based | Residence-based (worldwide) | **Territorial** | **Territorial** |
| **Foreign income taxed?** | Likely yes (residence-based) | **Yes** (but US SS exempt per treaty) | ❌ **No** | ❌ **No** |
| **Top income tax rate** | 33.3% | 35% | 25% | 25% |
| **Capital gains tax** | No | Yes (varies) | **No** | **Generally exempt** |
| **US tax treaty?** | **No** | ✅ **Yes (1992)** — SS only taxed in US | Yes (limited) | **No** |
| **Totalization agreement?** | No | No (2004 draft never ratified) | No | No |
| **VAT / IVA** | 14% | 16% (8% border zone) | 7% | 13% |
| **Property tax (annual)** | ~0.5-1% | **<$200/yr (predial)** | 0-2% cadastral | **0.25% assessed** |
| **Property transfer tax** | Varies | Varies by state | Varies | 1.5% |
| **Tax filing complexity (US expat)** | High (no treaty) | Moderate (treaty, but worldwide taxation) | Low (territorial + limited treaty) | Moderate (territorial but no treaty) |

**Winner: Panama** (territorial + capital gains exempt + US tax treaty). Costa Rica is a close second (territorial, simpler rates) but lacks a US tax treaty which adds complexity on the US filing side.

⚠️ Both Mexico and Guyana tax worldwide income once you're tax-resident. Panama and Costa Rica only tax local-source income — a massive advantage for retirees living off US pensions, Social Security, and investment income.

## 10. THE OIL BOOM X-FACTOR (Guyana)

Guyana is experiencing one of the world's fastest-growing economies due to massive offshore oil discoveries by ExxonMobil. GDP grew 62.3% in 2022 and continues at double-digit rates. This creates:

- **Upside:** Rapidly improving infrastructure, new hotels, expanding airport, growing middle class, potential real estate appreciation
- **Downside:** Inflation in expat-relevant goods/services, construction boom causing disruption, "Dutch disease" risk, inequality tension
- **Investment angle:** Could be a frontier real-estate play — but NOT a comfortable retirement destination yet

For an entrepreneurial retiree with local ties and a high risk tolerance, Guyana could be a 10-year bet. For a typical retiree wanting peace, healthcare, and community, it's premature.

## TAKE / NEGOTIATE / PASS

### 🇵🇦 Panama — **TAKE** (if you have the $1,000/mo pension)
The most balanced package. Pensionado benefits are real money (20-50% off everything from flights to doctor visits). The territorial tax system means your US retirement income is untaxed locally. The US dollar eliminates currency risk. Healthcare (private) is excellent. Property rights are full and equal. The only real downside is that Panama City is hot and humid — but Boquete solves that.

👉 **Ideal for:** Retirees with a pension who want the best structural deal, minimal tax friction, and don't need a massive expat social scene.

### 🇨🇷 Costa Rica — **TAKE** (if healthcare and safety are your top priority)
Best healthcare in the region, safest country, and the Pensionado visa is the most accessible ($1,000/mo, 1 day/year minimum stay). Territorial tax means foreign income is tax-free. The catch: beachfront property ownership is restricted, and overall cost of living is the highest of the four. No US tax treaty adds complexity on the US filing side.

👉 **Ideal for:** Health-focused retirees who want universal coverage, nature/biodiversity, maximum safety, and don't mind the rainy season.

### 🇲🇽 Mexico — **TAKE** (if community and climate variety matter most)
The largest, most established expat infrastructure anywhere. Cheapest day-to-day living. Unmatched climate variety — you can live in eternal spring at Lake Chapala, colonial charm in San Miguel, or Caribbean beaches. The visa threshold is the highest ($4,400/mo or $74K savings), which locks out some retirees. The IMSS chronic disease exclusion is a serious healthcare gap. But if you're healthy and income-qualified, the expat experience is unparalleled.

👉 **Ideal for:** Sociable retirees who want a proven expat ecosystem, short flights to the US, and value climate choice and low costs above all else.

### 🇬🇾 Guyana — **PASS** (for most retirees)
Guyana is not a retirement destination in the same league as the other three. No dedicated retiree visa, poor healthcare infrastructure, elevated crime, minimal expat community, and import-inflated living costs. The English language and oil-boom investment angle are the only draws. For a standard retiree seeking comfort, community, healthcare, and value, it's a clear fourth. 

👉 **Only consider if:** You have family or business ties to Guyana, are comfortable with frontier-market risk, or are making a speculative real-estate play on the oil boom. For 95% of US retirees, cross it off.
