## 6. Verdict

**Take — even the conservative scenario clears an 8% stabilized cash-on-cash hurdle by a wide margin, and a 5-year IRR north of 30% with a ~3x MOIC on an $8K acquisition is exceptional.** The arithmetic works because the city has discounted the land below replacement cost of the structure; almost all the value comes from the rehab + lease-up, not the purchase.

The risks are operational, not financial:
1. **Rehab surprises** on a 1896 brick (lead, asbestos, knob-and-tube, foundation) can blow the $60-80K budget. The 10% contingency helps but a 25-50% overrun is plausible.
2. **12-month CO covenant** is a hard deadline — missing it can trigger reversion on city deals. Plan permits + contractor mobilization **before** closing.
3. **Lease-up** in a C+/B- corridor is the real swing factor. Pre-leasing the retail (ideally to an established operator — coffee, salon, mission-driven tenant) before stabilization is the single biggest value-protect lever.

**Recommendation:** Bid $8K asking, with soft costs reserve of $5-10K added to the conservative all-in. Pursue Wisconsin historic credits (5% state + 20% federal on qualifying rehab) as upside — they are not modeled in this baseline.

## 7. Key Risk Notes

- **Quit-claim deed** — no seller reps; you accept all defects. Inspection contingency before close is essentially decorative, but do a Phase I ESA anyway (~$2-3K).
- **Property tax re-assessment** post-rehab is the largest hidden OpEx swing. Model uses 75-110% bump from acquisition basis ($2K/yr); verify with City Assessor before bidding.
- **Cap rate sensitivity** — every 50 bps move in cap rate moves ARV by 6-7%. The 8.5% (moderate) is a defensible central estimate for N. Division mixed-use.
- **Residential vouchers** — North Division has strong Section 8 demand. Voucher tenants de-risk lease-up but typically cap appraised value at 9-10% cap.
- **Soft costs not modeled** — architect, permits, structural/Phase I engineering ≈ $3-8K; add to conservative all-in if going with a 5-yr hold.
- **Refi exit** — once stabilized at Y2-3, a 75% LTV refi returns ~60-70% of invested capital while keeping the cash flow. Likely real-world exit.
