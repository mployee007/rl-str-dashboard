# Summary — Chicago Curbside EV Charger Rules

**Session:** 2026-11-03
**Question:** Can you install a Level 1 or Level 2 EV charger outside at the curb in residential Chicago?
**Answer:** No at the curb. Yes on your own property, with a permit.

## The three findings that drive the answer

**1. The curb is not a permitting problem.** The sidewalk, parkway, and curb are City right-of-way. Chicago issues public-way permits for awnings, signs, café seating, and construction — not for privately owned EV charging equipment. There is no application to file.

**2. Chicago passed a curbside charging program in 2026, but it is not for residents.** Ordinance O2026-0025394 passed City Council on 7/15/2026, inserting Municipal Code § 9-64-225. It authorizes the CDOT Commissioner to install and operate curbside chargers — but defines an eligible station as equipment *"owned by the City of Chicago and operated for public use."* The ordinance contains no resident-application pathway and sunsets on June 30, 2030. This is the City building public infrastructure, not homeowners building private chargers.

**3. The extension-cord workaround is a code violation, not a gray area.** Chicago Electrical Code § 14X-7-705.3 prohibits using extension cords as a substitute for permanent wiring, affixing them to structures, subjecting them to environmental damage, using indoor-rated cords outdoors, and using cords for anything but portable appliances. A car charging overnight across a sidewalk from a front porch outlet fails on at least four of those at once — before you get to sidewalk obstruction, ADA, and liability.

## What actually works for a Chicago resident without a driveway

| Path | Practicality |
|---|---|
| Rent/find off-street parking with an outlet | Best. A garage space solves the problem entirely. |
| Illinois right-to-charge (renters) | Strong. Statutory right to install with written landlord approval; landlord cannot unreasonably deny. |
| Condo/HOA installation | Strong. Association cannot unreasonably restrict; owner pays, owns, and insures. |
| Curb cut / new parking pad | Possible, expensive, aldermanic approval, restricted on many residential blocks. |
| City curbside pilot | Advocate only. 311 and your alderman. No application exists. |
| ComEd curbside pilot | Not applicable — Dixon, Oswego, Sandwich only. |

## Practical spec to build to

Chicago defines an EVSE-ready space as having conduit and wiring for a **40-amp, 208/240V dedicated branch circuit terminating within 3 feet of the parking space.** Build to that even if installing Level 1 now — it's the difference between a $300 job and a $3,000 one later.

## Consequences of doing it unpermitted

Fine of **200% of the initial permit fee**, plus **$200–500 per day** the property is in violation.
