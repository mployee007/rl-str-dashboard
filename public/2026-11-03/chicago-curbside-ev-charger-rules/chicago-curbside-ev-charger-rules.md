# Can You Install an EV Charger at the Curb in Residential Chicago?

**Prepared:** 2026-11-03
**Verdict:** **No** at the curb for a private resident — but **yes** on your own property with a permit, and Chicago now has a city-run curbside pilot worth knowing about.

---

## 1. The short answer

| Location | Level 1 | Level 2 | Reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Curb / parkway / sidewalk (public way)** | ❌ No | ❌ No | City right-of-way. No permit pathway exists for private equipment. |
| **Your driveway or garage** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | Building/electrical permit + licensed contractor |
| **Parking pad on your lot (front, side, or alley)** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | Permit; may need zoning relief for the pad itself |
| **Shared lot / multi-family building** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | Zoning requires EVSE-ready spaces in newer buildings |
| **Rental unit** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | Illinois EV Charging Act gives you a right to install |
| **Condo / HOA** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | Association cannot unreasonably restrict you |

The curb is not a permitting problem you can solve with the right paperwork. It is a legal boundary — the sidewalk, parkway, and curb belong to the City of Chicago, and Chicago does not issue public-way permits for privately owned EV charging equipment.

---

## 2. What changed in 2026 — the curbside pilot

Most articles you'll find online are out of date on this. On **July 15, 2026**, City Council passed **O2026-0025394**, creating **Municipal Code § 9-64-225, "Electric Vehicle Public Charging Pilot Program."**

What it actually does:

- Defines an eligible charging station as equipment **"owned by the City of Chicago and operated for public use."** That single phrase is why this is not a resident program.
- Authorizes the CDOT Commissioner to **install, operate, and maintain — or contract with others to install, operate, and maintain** — curbside charging stations on the public way and in public parking lots.
- Lets the Traffic Compliance Administrator **set fees**, variable by hour and day of week.
- Authorizes agreements: contracts, revenue-sharing, data-sharing.
- **Sunsets June 30, 2030.**

**There is no resident application process in the ordinance.** It is the City building public charging, not homeowners installing private charging. So your realistic lever is political, not administrative: 311 requests and your alderman, aimed at getting your block on CDOT's list.

Separately, ComEd runs its own curbside make-ready pilot — but its participating municipalities are **Dixon, Oswego, and Sandwich**. Chicago is not one of them.

## 3. Why the extension cord to the curb doesn't work

This is the workaround everyone tries, and in Chicago it fails on the code, not just on etiquette.

**Chicago Electrical Code § 14X-7-705.3 (Extension cords)** prohibits, in plain terms:

- Using an extension cord **as a substitute for permanent wiring**
- **Affixing cords to structures**
- Subjecting cords to **environmental damage or physical impact**
- Using **indoor-rated cords outdoors**
- Using cords for anything other than **portable appliances** — a car is not a portable appliance
- Running cords **under doors or floor coverings**

Add sidewalk obstruction rules, ADA requirements, and personal liability if a delivery driver trips over your cord at night, and the picture is clear.

## 4. What you can actually do

### If you own the property and have off-street space

This is the straightforward path and it works.

1. **Licensed Illinois electrical contractor** — required by law.
2. **City of Chicago building/electrical permit** — EV charger installation and maintenance must be done by a licensed contractor under permit.
3. **Meet the Chicago standard.** The City defines an "EVSE-ready space" as having conduit and wiring for a **40-ampere, 208- or 240-volt dedicated branch circuit terminating within 3 feet of the parking space.** Build to that spec even if you're installing Level 1 today.
4. **Inspection** after install.

**Penalties for skipping the permit:** a fine of **200% of the initial permit fee**, plus **$200–500 per day** the property remains in violation. The City targets permit issuance within 5 business days of a complete application and inspections within 10 business days of a request.

**Level 1 vs. Level 2 in practice:**

| | Level 1 | Level 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit | Standard 120V, 15/20A | 240V, 40A dedicated |
| Adds to a Model Y | ~3–5 miles/hour | ~25–35 miles/hour |
| Typical work | Existing outdoor GFCI outlet, often no new circuit | New dedicated circuit, conduit run, possibly panel upgrade |
| Chicago permit | Yes, still required | Yes |
| Best for | Overnight top-ups, low daily mileage | Daily commuters, two-car households |

If your panel is already near capacity — common in Chicago two-flats and greystones — budget for a service upgrade or an energy-management system. Chicago's code explicitly allows EVSE energy management systems to reduce required capacity for EVSE-installed spaces (but not for EVSE-ready spaces).

### If you want a parking pad where there wasn't one

A **curb cut** is possible in theory and brutal in practice: CDOT driveway permit, zoning relief, aldermanic sign-off, and Chicago restricts new curb cuts in many residential blocks. Get a quote on the whole scope before you fall in love with the idea.

### If you rent

The **Illinois Electric Vehicle Charging Act (765 ILCS 1085, Public Act 103-0053)** gives you a statutory right to install EVSE at your rented residence:

- You must obtain **written landlord approval**
- The landlord **cannot unreasonably deny** the request
- **You pay** for the installation
- The equipment **belongs to you**, not the property
- You must typically **restore** the property at lease end

### If you're in a condo or HOA

The same Act governs associations:

- The association **cannot unreasonably restrict** installation or use of a charging system in your unit or your deeded/designated parking space
- If approval is required, the association must process it like any other alteration request and **cannot unreasonably delay or deny** it
- The system installed by a unit owner **is the unit owner's property** and is not part of the common elements
- Expect to carry insurance naming the association, and to comply with architectural standards

### If you're buying or renovating

For building permit applications filed on or after **November 1, 2023**, **100% of off-street parking spaces serving dwelling units must be EVSE-ready or EVSE-installed.** Affordable housing developments can get a reduction. This matters if you're shopping for a newer building — the infrastructure is already there.

## 5. Money

- **ComEd** offers EV charger and installation rebates — check current program terms before buying equipment.
- **Federal 30C credit** (30% of cost, up to $1,000) is still listed on Chicago's own EV page, **but verify before counting on it** — the federal credit was terminated for property placed in service after June 30, 2026 under the 2025 tax legislation. Treat it as likely unavailable now.
- **Illinois EPA EV rebate** cycles open periodically — the City's page referenced an August 1, 2026 cycle opening.

---

## 6. Bottom line for a Chicago resident without a driveway

1. **You cannot put a private charger at the curb.** No permit exists for it, and an extension cord across the sidewalk violates the electrical code, the sidewalk obstruction rules, and ADA.
2. **Your real options, in order of practicality:** rent or find off-street parking with an outlet → negotiate an EVSE install with your landlord under the Illinois right-to-charge law → pursue a curb cut if you own the property and have the budget → push your alderman for a spot in the City's curbside pilot.
3. **The City's pilot is real but it is public charging, not yours.** It will show up where CDOT decides to put it, subject to appropriation. You can advocate; you cannot apply.
