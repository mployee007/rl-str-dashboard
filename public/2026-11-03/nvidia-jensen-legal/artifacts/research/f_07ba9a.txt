Jensen Huang in 2023

In October 2026, _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_New\_York\_Times "The New York Times")_ reported that [Jensen Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang "Jensen Huang"), the chief executive of [Nvidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia "Nvidia"), had been the target of an espionage plot involving the [government of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_China "Government of China"). Huang was involved in an [extramarital affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extramarital_affair "Extramarital affair") with Alice Huang (no familial connection[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-NYTimesTarget-1)), a Chinese executive of a [Singaporean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") company, who had allegedly been recruited by China's [Ministry of State Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_State_Security_(China) "Ministry of State Security (China)") to serve as a spy.

Near the end of [Donald Trump's first presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_presidency_of_Donald_Trump "First presidency of Donald Trump"), a growing number of Chinese companies became domiciled in Singapore, a practice termed "Singapore washing", which has since accelerated, spreading to various sectors from [critical minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_raw_materials "Critical raw materials") to tech and [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology "Biotechnology"). With 28 [free trade agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_agreement "Free trade agreement"), Singapore was considered a good base to grow markets outside China.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-kokfu-2)

Megaspeed is a Singaporean [data center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_center_services "Data center services") firm. The company was incorporated in 2015 as 7Road International, owned by a Chinese investment holding company involved in the video game industry. It grew substantially after President [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") imposed restrictions on the sale of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") hardware to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") in October 2022. 7Road International changed its name to Megaspeed International in March 2023. The Chinese split off the resulting company,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-kokfu-2) which was sold to Huang Le Limited. In October, 2023 Huang Le ended an eight-month tenure as managing director of Megaspeed, when the company was sold to a Singaporean company owned by Tan Yong Pong, who became Megaspeed's chief executive.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-kok-3) Four days after the sale, Megaspeed became an [Nvidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia "Nvidia") Cloud Partner.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-4) By 2025, Megaspeed faced a U.S. probe for allegedly diverting Nvidia chips used for AI.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-kokfu-2)

As early as June 2024, Jensen Huang had begun a close relationship with Alice Huang (Chinese name: Huáng Lè; 黄乐). According to _The New York Times_, Alice Huang, a Chinese executive at Megaspeed, had allegedly been recruited by China's [Ministry of State Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_State_Security_(China) "Ministry of State Security (China)") (MSS) to serve as a spy.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-NYTimesTarget-1)

### Federal investigation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Federal investigation")]

As early as September 2024, the [United States Department of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Commerce "United States Department of Commerce")'s [Bureau of Industry and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Industry_and_Security "Bureau of Industry and Security") had begun investigating Megaspeed and Alice Huang.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-5) In August 2025, Tim Teter, Nvidia's general counsel, contacted officials at the bureau to request a meeting. Teter stated that he had received permission from [Jeffrey I. Kessler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_I._Kessler "Jeffrey I. Kessler"), the [under secretary of commerce for industry and security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Commerce_for_Industry_and_Security "Under Secretary of Commerce for Industry and Security"), to discuss the case. The officials declined Teter's offer and forwarded the email to Kessler, who said that they should continue the inquiry lawfully. In an effort to prevent the investigation from being compromised, the inquiry was transferred to the [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice "United States Department of Justice"), which opened a criminal investigation into Megaspeed.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-NYTimesInquiry-6)

### Corporate investigation

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Corporate investigation")]

After _The New York Times_ reported that Huang had done business with a Chinese spy, Nvidia opened an investigation into Jensen Huang.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-7)

### United States government

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: United States government")]

[![Image 2](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/P20250430JB-0891.jpg/250px-P20250430JB-0891.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:P20250430JB-0891.jpg)

Jensen Huang (right) and President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") (left) in April 2025

According to _The New York Times_, President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") had been briefed on the spying scandal in October 2025, but chose not to take action.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-NYTimesInquiry-6) It also reported that Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Rubio "Marco Rubio") had drafted a highly classified memo in spring 2026 warning of Huang's relationship, which was circulated to government officials with high-level security clearances before [Trump's visit to China in May 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_state_visit_by_Donald_Trump_to_China "2026 state visit by Donald Trump to China"), which Huang joined.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-8) Jensen Huang had a close relationship to Trump, which developed after Huang met the president at [Mar-a-Lago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar-a-Lago "Mar-a-Lago") and donated to the [White House State Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_State_Ballroom "White House State Ballroom"). Trump had previously refused to take actions that would disrupt Nvidia's sales—which had been bolstering the economy—or the United States's trade relationship with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-NYTimesTarget-1) At a [Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween "Halloween") party at Mar-a-Lago, Trump stated that he wanted Huang to remain on the [President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President's_Council_of_Advisors_on_Science_and_Technology "President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology"), describing Huang as "very above board".[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-9)

[Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida") representative [Brian Mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Mast "Brian Mast"), the chair of the [House Committee on Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs "United States House Committee on Foreign Affairs"); [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan") representative [John Moolenaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Moolenaar "John Moolenaar"), the chair of the [House Select Committee on Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Select_Committee_on_Strategic_Competition_between_the_United_States_and_the_Chinese_Communist_Party "United States House Select Committee on Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party"); and [Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas "Arkansas") representative [Rick Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Crawford_(politician) "Rick Crawford (politician)"), the chair of the [House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Permanent_Select_Committee_on_Intelligence "United States House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence"), requested that Huang appear before a closed-door briefing in November 2026.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-10)

On 9 November, [CIA director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_director "CIA director")[John Ratcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ratcliffe "John Ratcliffe") and [Director of National Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_National_Intelligence "Director of National Intelligence")[Jay Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Clayton "Jay Clayton") gave a closed-door briefing about Huang's affair to U.S. congressional leaders.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-11) The same day, Trump stated that the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation "Federal Bureau of Investigation") (FBI) was investigating Huang's affair.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-Boak2026-1-12)

On November 8, 2026, Jensen Huang sent an apology to Nvidia employees admitting to marital infidelity.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-13)

On November 9, 2026, Nvidia's stock fell as much as eight percent in early trading[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-14) before gaining to a loss of five percent amid a broad market rally.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-15) The investigation occurred as concerns about the artificial intelligence industry mounted, contributing to Nvidia losing US$1.5 trillion in market capitalization within over a month.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-16) That same day, investors filed a [class action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_action "Class action") lawsuit against Nvidia and Jensen Huang, accusing them of fraud for concealing Huang's ties with a suspected Chinese spy.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-17)

China's [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_(China) "Ministry of Foreign Affairs (China)") stated that _The New York Times_ report was "pure fabrication out of thin air."[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-18)[Guo Jiakun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guo_Jiakun "Guo Jiakun") called the report "smears against China."[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-Boak2026-1-12) Some Chinese [state media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_media "State media") reports have claimed that _The New York Times_ report was aimed at destabilizing [U.S.–China relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93United_States_relations "China–United States relations").[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-Boak2026-1-12)

### Singapore government

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Singapore government")]

In October 2025, [Singapore police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Police_Force "Singapore Police Force") were investigating Megaspeed over "suspected breaches of our domestic laws". In October 2026, the [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Home_Affairs_(Singapore) "Ministry of Home Affairs (Singapore)") stated no investigations were conducted in ​respect of Alice ⁠Huang, while investigations into Megaspeed continued. Singapore said it did not enforce "unilateral export controls imposed by other countries", however it acts "firmly against companies and individuals who violate our laws and are engaged in fraudulent and dishonest practices".[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang_spying_scandal#cite_note-kok-3)
