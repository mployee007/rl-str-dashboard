## 0. Executive Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is childcare profitable in Chicago? | **Yes, but the profit lives in the home/group-home tier, not big centers.** |
| Best entry for a first-time owner | **Licensed Daycare Home (8 kids) or Group Home (12 kids).** Near-zero startup, high owner income, no commercial lease. |
| Center (30–60 kids)? | Only viable with **85%+ sustained enrollment** and disciplined staffing; thin 5–10% margins and $400K+ upfront. |
| Payback | Home ≈ **1–2 months**. Group home ≈ **<6 months**. Center ≈ **8–10 years**. |
| Verdict | **Enter small (home → group home), treat the center as an expansion after 12–18 months of full waitlists.** |

The single most important fact in this report: **a 40-child Chicago center loses money below ~80% enrollment.** That is the #1 reason new centers fail — owners sign a long lease and staff up before enrollment ramps. The capital-light tiers (home, group home) sidestep that risk entirely.

## 1. How to Start a Daycare in Chicago (Licensing & Compliance)

### 1.1 Who regulates you
Illinois is unusual: child care is licensed by the **Department of Children & Family Services (DCFS)**, not DHS. Licensing authority is currently moving to the new **Illinois Department of Early Childhood (IDEC)** — check `idec.illinois.gov` for the latest, but the substance of the rules is unchanged.
You'll also need City of Chicago permits (see 1.4).

### 1.2 Do you need a license?
**Yes — a DCFS license is required to care for more than 3 unrelated children under age 12 (including your own children) for less than 24 hours/day.** Caring for 3 or fewer children is license-exempt, but a license-unexempt provider **cannot accept CCAP subsidies** and can't legally scale.

### 1.3 The three license types

| License | Capacity | Setting | Your fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day Care Home** | up to **8** children | your residence | Best low-cost start; owner-operated |
| **Group Day Care Home** | up to **12** children (16 incl. your own under 12) | your residence (larger) | Sweet spot: real staff + real profit |
| **Day Care Center** | 3+ children | commercial space | Only with capital + a director on staff |

### 1.4 Application process (DCFS)

**Day Care Home / Group Home:**
1. Complete the free **online licensing orientation** (sunshine.dcfs.illinois.gov).
2. Submit the child care facility license application **+ authorization forms for background checks** on all staff and every household member 13+.
3. Schedule a **home visit** with a licensing rep (assesses safety, space, health).
4. Receive license — **valid 3 years**.

**Day Care Center:**
1. Contact your **local DCFS licensing office** before applying (the process is complex).
2. Submit application + articles of incorporation/bylaws (if a corp) + background-check authorizations.
3. Receive a **6-month conditional license**; a rep visits regularly.
4. If compliant → full license, **valid 3 years**.

### 1.5 Key requirements (all license types)

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **License fee** | **$0** (free) through DCFS |
| **Background checks** | Criminal + child abuse/neglect (CANTS) for every caregiver, assistant, and household member 13+, via the DCFS portal |
| **Health report** | Current physical for provider |
| **Training** | Pre-service training + **15 clock-hours of in-service training every licensing year** |
| **Staff qualifications** | Director/teacher: 60 semester hrs w/ 18 in child development, OR 2 yrs experience + 30 hrs + proof of enrollment, OR Montessori credential, etc. |
| **Safety** | Radon testing, first-aid/CPR, safe-sleep & sanitation standards |
| **Renewal** | Forms arrive 6 mo before expiry; file 3 mo before |

### 1.6 Staff-to-child ratios (DCFS — the cost driver)
| Classroom | Ratio | Max group |
|---|---|---|
| Infant (6 wk–15 mo) | **1 : 4** | 12 |
| Toddler (15–23 mo) | **1 : 5** | — |
| 2 years | 1 : 8 | 16 |
| Preschool (3–5) | **1 : 10** | 20 |
| School age | 1 : 20 | 30 |

> Ratios are the economics. Infant care needs 1 adult per 4 kids — that's why infant tuition is highest (Chicago ~$1,907/mo).

### 1.7 City of Chicago & local layer
- **Chicago business license** (Dept. of Business Affairs & Consumer Protection, BACP).
- **Food service sanitation certification** (City of Chicago + Cook County) if you prepare/serve food.
- **Zoning + building permit** and **City fire inspection** for centers (commercial use, occupancy, fire suppression).
- Home providers: check **zoning** for home-based business + your landlord/HOA if you rent.

### Chicago & Illinois tuition (full-time)
| Age | Chicago center (Willow Tree, actual) | Illinois average |
|---|---|---|
| Infant | **$1,907/mo** (extended day up to $2,542) | $1,650/mo ($19,800/yr) |
| Toddler | $1,676/mo | — |
| Twos | $1,791/mo | — |
| Preschool | — | $1,437/mo ($17,240/yr) |

- **National** licensed-center average: **$332/wk ≈ $1,440/mo** (Care.com, 2025).
- **CCAP reimbursement (Cook Co., Group 1A): up to ~$67/day for an infant** at a licensed center ≈ **$1,450/mo** — a reliable, subsidized revenue stream that doesn't cannibalize private-pay.
- **Chicago nanny** benchmark: ~$22/hr (Cheapest substitute — caps how high you can price).
- 20% of families spend **>$30K/yr**; 78% spend **≥10% of income** (Care.com) → strong willingness-to-pay, but also a price ceiling.

## 4. ROI & Return on Investment (modeled)

### 4.1 Licensed Day Care Home (8 kids, owner-operated)
| Scenario | Enrolled | Revenue/mo | Opex/mo | **Net/mo** | **Net/yr (owner income)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (55%) | 4.4 | $6,380 | $987 | **$5,393** | **$64,716** |
| Moderate (75%) | 6 | $9,300 | $1,075 | **$8,225** | **$98,700** |
| Optimistic (95%) | 7.6 | $12,920 | $1,163 | **$11,757** | **$141,084** |

- Startup **$7K** → payback **<2 months**. 
- ⚠️ "Net" here ≈ **your own wages** for working full-time as the caregiver — this is a *job you buy for $7K*, not passive profit. Treat home daycare as a high-paying self-employment role (~$64K–$141K/yr income), not a scalable business you can step away from.

### 4.2 Licensed Group Day Care Home (12 kids, you + 1 assistant)
| Scenario | Enrolled | Revenue/mo | Opex/mo | **Net/mo** | **Net/yr** | Owner equiv. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (55%) | 7 | $9,240 | $3,703 | **$5,537** | **$66,446** | ~$26/hr |
| Moderate (75%) | 9 | $13,950 | $3,835 | **$10,115** | **$121,382** | ~$47/hr |
| Optimistic (95%) | 11 | $19,380 | $3,967 | **$15,413** | **$184,958** | ~$71/hr |

- Startup **~$18K** → payback **<6 months**. **This is the highest-leverage model** — employees are paid (so it's a "real" business), margins hold 60–80%, and owner income scales past $150K/yr with $71/hr-equivalent.

### 4.3 Licensed Day Care Center (40 children, rented 5,200 sf)
| Scenario | Enrolled | Revenue/mo | Opex/mo | **Net/mo** | **Net/yr** | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (55%) | 23 | $38,729 | $57,601 | **-$18,873** | **-$226,474** | -49% |
| Moderate (75%) | 31 | $52,434 | $59,708 | **-$7,274** | **-$87,290** | -14% |
| Optimistic (95%) | 39 | $66,139 | $61,815 | **+$4,325** | **+$51,894** | +6.5% |

- Startup **$484K** → payback **9.3 years** even at 95% enrollment. 
- **Beware:** below ~80–85% enrollment the center *bleeds* $7K–$19K/month. Labor is ~55% of cost (DCFS ratios force heavy staffing), rent+utilities ~15%, food/supplies/insurance ~25%.

### 4.4 Why the difference is so stark
| Lever | Home / Group Home | Center |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed cost | virtually none (existing house) | lease + payroll from day 1 |
| Labor | owner's own time | 8 teachers + director + floater |
| Break-even enrollment | ~2–3 kids | ~33–35 kids |
| Capital | $7K–$18K | $150K–$484K |

**Recommendation: start with a Day Care Home, graduate to a Group Home when you have a waitlist, and only consider a center once you have a waitlist of 30+ and a director you'd trust to run it.**

## 5. Business Model

### 5.1 Core model — subscription childcare
Recurring monthly tuition per child (the "seat" is your inventory). Margins improve as classroom ratios climb with age, so **programs that fill their infant/toddler rooms subsidize the cheaper preschool rooms** — price accordingly.

### 5.2 Revenue streams (stack them)
1. **Private-pay tuition** — core (~$1,400–$1,900/mo/child in Chicago).
2. **CCAP (subsidized)** — accept Child Care Assistance Program; state pays up to ~$67/day in Cook Co. Reliable, fills empty seats.
3. **Registration/supply fees** — $100–$150/child one-time (captures a $100–$150 seat-holding cash flow).
4. **Extended care / early-drop-off / late-pickup** — $10–$15/hr, high-margin.
5. **Part-time & drop-in** — fill dead capacity.
6. **Enrichment add-ons** (music, Spanish, gymnastics) — upsell 10–20%.
7. **Preschool for All / Head Start** contracts — public funding (eligibility required).
8. **Employer backup-care contracts** — corporates pay premium for guaranteed slots.

### 5.3 Unit economics (group home, moderate)
- Revenue/child: ~$1,550/mo · Opex/child: ~$426/mo · **Contribution ~$1,124/child/mo**.
- Every added child above ~3 covers fixed costs, then flows ~72% to profit.

### 5.4 Cost structure (center, sourced ratios)
Labor **50–60%** · Occupancy **15–20%** · Food/materials **10–15%** · Insurance/admin/marketing **~10%**. Protect the labor line — it's both your quality moat and your profit killer.

## 6. Marketing Plan (Chicago-specific)

Chicago is **neighborhood-driven** — parents in Bucktown/Logan Square/Lincoln Park/Lakeview/Roscoe Village won't cross town for care. Win a ZIP code, not "Chicago."

### 6.1 Foundation (week 1–4)
- **Google Business Profile** + **Winnie** + **Yelp** listings with real photos of the space and staff.
- One-page waitlist landing page: "Infant & toddler care in [Neighborhood] — join the waitlist."
- Local SEO: pages for "daycare near [ZIP]" and "[neighborhood] preschool." Parents search hyper-locally.

### 6.2 Demand capture (month 1–3)
- **Tour funnel:** every inquiry → same-day response → booked tour within 72 hrs → 30–40% tour-to-enroll. Track it.
- **Waitlist nurture:** monthly email ("a spot opening in our 2s room in [month]") — daycares live and die on waitlists.
- **Referral engine:** current parents are your best channel — give $200–$300 tuition credit per enrolled referral.

### 6.3 Partnerships (month 2+, compounding)
- **Pediatricians & OB/GYNs** (Northwestern/Lurie/UChicago networks) — leave brochures; new parents decide in weeks 1–8.
- **Doulas, lactation consultants, new-parent groups, park districts**.
- **Employers** — pitch backup-care contracts to hospital systems and downtown firms.
- **Faith communities & Facebook/Nextdoor parent groups** — hyper-local word-of-mouth.

### 6.4 Differentiation that wins in Chicago
- **Bilingual Spanish** (huge demand in Chicago and differentiated).
- **Extended hours** for health-care/shift workers (Rush, Northwestern, Stroger staff).
- **Montessori / Reggio Emilia** positioning — lets you justify top-of-market tuition.
- **CCAP-friendly** — market to subsidized families; fills rooms and is steady revenue.

### 6.5 Budget
- Home/group home: **$60–$80/mo** (flyers, boost occasional posts). Growth is referral-driven.
- Center: **$800/mo launch, then $300–$500/mo** (Google Local Services Ads + retargeting + referral).

## 7. Risks & Mitigation

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Center loses money <80% enrollment | High | Start home/group home; open center only on waitlist depth |
| Staffing shortages (the industry's #1 pain) | High | Pay above market; hire-before-you-scale; treat teachers as partners |
| Regulatory (DCFS + City backlog, radon, fire) | Medium | Build 3–6 mo into timeline; front-load inspections |
| Insurance cost/denial | Medium | Specialty child care carrier; umbrella policy |
| Price ceiling (nanny ~$22/hr; 20% pay >$30K/yr) | Medium | Add CCAP + enrichment to diversify price points |
| Single-point-of-failure (owner illness) | Medium | Group home has an assistant; cross-train from day 1 |

## 8. Action Plan (next 30 days)
1. **Decide tier** — recommend Day Care Home or Group Home (not center).
2. Complete DCFS **online orientation** (free).
3. Run **background checks** on all adults in the household.
4. Order **radon test** + fire/safety walkthrough; verify **zoning**.
5. File **LLC** (Sugalock LLC or a new childcare entity) + **insurance**.
6. Build **Google Business Profile + waitlist page** before you're licensed (capture demand early).
7. Set **tuition** off Willow Tree / neighborhood comps (don't underprice — scarcity is real).
